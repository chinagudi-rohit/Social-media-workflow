# Jake Trinder: "Give Me 15 Minutes and I'll Make You Disgustingly Good at YouTube"

youtu.be/Dx-c19qXdxM, keynote recording, 14 Oct 2026, 403K views. Studied 2026-10-26
from youtubesummary.com's summary of the captions and Higgsfield's scene analysis. YouTube
blocked the sandbox's download, so no frames were watched.

His thesis: a video is a series system. Topic, packaging, opening, delivery and review all
have to work, and one bad decision sinks it. Better decisions come from research.

| His step | Our version | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Revealed preference: topics people already watch. Estimate a topic's floor (a decent video's views) and ceiling (the best video's). | Competitor outliers (ceiling), now with a small-channel boost. | None. Floor needs view data from more channels. |
| Raise the ceiling by joining a proven topic to a bigger name or angle (lead generation + Hormozi). | "Borrowed authority" in templates/script_guide.md. | None |
| Packaging patterns across several examples, never one; know *why* a format works before copying it (avoid "overfitting"). | Viral audit + outliers. | None |
| The opening answers: is this what I clicked, why you, how, is it for me. | The spoken hook and `on_screen_hook` confirm the promise in 2-5 s. | None |
| Treat the title's promise as acceptance criteria: list what must be true, then make sure each item is delivered. | Nothing checked this. | **video-reviewer FIX rule:** the video delivers what the title and hook promise. |
| Optimize satisfaction, not retention alone (12% retention, 800K views). | Insights tracked views and watch time. | **Autopilot 1c:** also compare likes, comments and follows per view, and read metrics as clues, not verdicts. |

Exaggeration check: the 12%-retention example is long-form training content. For Shorts,
the swipe-away rate still dominates (data/insights/lessons.md, 2026-10-22).
