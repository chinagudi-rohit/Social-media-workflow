# Shorts / Reels reach research for "Worth Knowing" (2026-10-18)

Scope: a new faceless, AI-made explainer channel (finance, credit, insurance, real estate,
investing, AI tools, how things are made, history, space/science, psychology) whose goal is
maximum reach on YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels and Facebook Reels.

- **Evidence tags.**
  - **[Official]**: YouTube or Meta statement, help page or blog. Usually reached through
    news coverage, which is noted.
  - **[Data]**: a vendor or third-party dataset with a stated sample.
  - **[Practitioner]**: blog or creator opinion.

## 1. YouTube Shorts algorithm (2025-2026)

### 1.1 What drives impressions

- **Viewed vs swiped away (VVSA) is the Shorts equivalent of click-through rate.** Shorts have
  no thumbnail click, so the first signal is whether a viewer stays or swipes.
  - Todd Sherman (Shorts product lead) said so in a Rene Ritchie-hosted Q&A, as reported by
    OutlierKit and vidIQ. [Official, via secondary]
    https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/ ;
    https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/
  - A Short that underperforms its feed peers does not go much beyond its first,
    impression-like views. [Official, via secondary]
    https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/
- **Explore, then exploit.** A new Short goes to a small seed audience; if it beats its peers,
  it is shown more widely. Sherman described this model, as summarized by
  https://crklr.com/news/how-to-optimise-youtube-shorts-for-seo/ . [Official, via secondary]
- **Signals YouTube has named:**
  - whether viewers chose to view;
  - average view duration;
  - average percentage viewed;
  - likes;
  - satisfaction signals.

  These come from Bytecap's evidence synthesis, which cites YouTube's discovery guidance:
  https://www.bytecap.io/research/best-hooks-for-youtube-shorts . YouTube publishes no
  weights. [Official, via secondary]
- **"The audience is the algorithm."** Sherman's line, quoted by
  https://www.leptidigital.fr/reseaux-sociaux/fonctionnement-algorithme-youtube-shorts-46569/
- **No ideal duration or posting frequency.** Sherman told creators there is none, per
  https://ppc.world/news/youtube-obyasnil-kak-rabotayut-algoritmy-rekomendaciy-shorts/ . He
  also said recommendations are "independent of duration", quoted by
  https://piktochart.com/blog/how-long-youtube-shorts/ . [Official, via secondary]
- **The largest public VVSA dataset is Paddy Galloway's 2023 study** (33 channels, about 5,400
  Shorts). [Data, 2023, correlational]
  - Shorts with a 70-90% viewed rate performed best.
  - Shorts under 60% generally did not.
  - Advice: keep VVSA above 60% "but don't obsess".
  - "The first second" decides the swipe.

  Sources: https://videogen.io/blog/decoding-the-youtube-shorts-algorithm-a-deep-dive-into-3-3-billion-views ;
  https://www.digitalinformationworld.com/2023/04/paddy-galloway-decoded-youtube-shorts.html
  - The 70% "target" that many blogs repeat is a creator rule of thumb, not a YouTube
    threshold. https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/
- **Scale of Shorts:**
  - The Shorts feed drove **61%** of the views Metricool measured. [Data]
  - Average Short view duration fell to **about 16 s**, from about 48 s, across a 2025-to-2026
    comparison that straddles the change in view counting.
  - Subscribers drove about 11% of views, search about 9%, channel pages about 3% (all formats).

  Metricool 2026 study, 799,718 videos from 71,177 accounts, Feb 2025 vs Feb 2026:
  https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/08/03/youtube-shorts-long-form-study-metricool/ ;
  https://metricool.com/press-release-youtube-study-2026/
- **Tubular Labs:** 77% of global YouTube views in 2025 came from videos under one minute
  (mostly Shorts). [Data] https://www.tvrev.com/news/tube-trends-over-75-of-youtube-views-came-from-shorts-in-2025-tubular
- **The October 2026 originality change targets re-uploads.** It is aimed at channels that
  mostly aggregate other creators' clips, not at original AI-generated visuals. Rene Ritchie's
  examples of what is *not* original are about narrating over someone else's clip. [Official,
  via news] https://ppc.land/re-uploaded-shorts-lose-reach-as-youtube-favours-original-clips/

### 1.2 How new channels get their first distribution

- **There is no official documentation for new channels.** The consistent explanation is the
  per-Short seed test above, which subscribers barely affect. [Practitioner]
  https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-shorts-no-views-fix ;
  https://fliki.ai/blog/youtube-shorts-not-getting-views
- **Small accounts grew fastest in 2026.** Accounts under 10K subscribers grew Shorts views by
  more than 200% and interactions by 42%. [Data]
  https://www.netinfluencer.com/youtube-views-are-rising-but-watch-time-and-ad-revenue-are-falling-study-finds/
- **Volume alone does not get a new channel going.** A creator posted 300 Shorts in 30 days on a
  new channel and got about 131K views and 318 subscribers; **228 of the 300 had fewer than 100
  views**. [Anecdote, second-hand summary]
  https://lilys.ai/en/notes/create-youtube-shorts-20260115/300-shorts-30-days-results
- **Views can come late.**
  - Socialinsider says Shorts success "can be delayed, sometimes by weeks". [Data or
    Practitioner] https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/how-long-does-it-take-for-yt-shorts-to-get-views/
  - Metricool: 83% of *interactions* happen within the first 10 days.
    https://metricool.com/press-release-youtube-study-2026/
  - Practical consequence: judge a Short at 48 h *and* again at 7-10 days.
- **Mixing many niches on one channel:**
  - No official statement says it hurts.
  - Guides argue that one niche gives the seed audience a clearer starting point. [Practitioner]
    https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/niching-down-youtube-shorts-success
  - **Unverified for this channel**; it is worth measuring through per-niche results.

### 1.3 Posting frequency and volume

- **Metricool 2026 (largest dataset).** [Data]
  - Individual Shorts perform best at 2-4 uploads a week.
  - **Monthly Shorts view totals keep rising with more uploads, with no clear ceiling.** Each
    upload is another lottery ticket in the feed.
  - 35 or more Shorts a month removes the lift to long-form videos, which does not matter here.

  https://metricool.com/youtube-trends/ ;
  https://www.netinfluencer.com/youtube-views-are-rising-but-watch-time-and-ad-revenue-are-falling-study-finds/
- **YouTube's public line is quality over quantity.** Sherman (2023): no number of posts
  guarantees success. [Official, via secondary]
  https://aituber.app/blog/how-many-youtube-shorts-to-post-a-day/
- **Practitioner recommendations range from 3-7 a week to 1-3 a day.**
  - About one a day for channels under 100K subscribers:
    https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/how-often-to-post-youtube-shorts
  - Start at one a day: https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/how-often-should-you-post-shorts-to-grow-faster
  - Several a day is fine "if all three are different":
    https://aituber.app/blog/how-many-youtube-shorts-to-post-a-day/
- **Daily upload limits exist but are not published.** They vary by channel history and
  verification. [Official] https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/57407?hl=en ;
  https://fast.io/resources/youtube-upload-limit.md
- **Zack D. Films reportedly posts 4-5 videos a day.** [Unverified]
  https://ivideonow.com/blog/why-zack-d-films-dominates-youtube-shorts-with-unsettling-3d-animation-content-en
- **Risk pattern:** one guide names "channels publishing 10+ near-identical videos per day" as a
  demonetization trigger. [Practitioner]
  https://air.io/en/monetization/youtube-monetization-policy-changes-2026-a-complete-dated-timeline

### 1.4 Length sweet spot (Shorts can run 3 minutes)

- **Limit and claims rule.** Shorts can run up to 3 min. A Short over 1 min with any Content ID
  claim, including on music, is blocked globally. [Official]
  https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en
- **No published performance study covers 90-180 s Shorts.** Nobody seems to have measured
  them. https://www.bytecap.io/research/best-youtube-shorts-length ;
  https://www.toptal.com/creator/post/youtube-shorts-length
- **Galloway / Inflow Network (2023, about 5,400 Shorts, before 3-minute Shorts).** [Data, old]
  - 50-60 s Shorts averaged about 1.7M views, about 22x the views of clips under 10 s.
  - Shorts with average view duration above 50 s averaged about 4.1M views.
  - 30-40 s was the most common length.

  https://videogen.io/blog/decoding-the-youtube-shorts-algorithm-a-deep-dive-into-3-3-billion-views ;
  https://piktochart.com/blog/how-long-youtube-shorts/
- **EasyViral (vendor, 500 faceless Shorts with 1M+ views in 2026).** [Data, method unpublished]
  - "Sweet spot" 20-45 s.
  - Single-fact videos peak around 13-18 s.
  - "If your script works in 25 seconds, don't pad it to 55."

  https://easyviral.ai/blog/we-analyzed-500-viral-faceless-shorts
- **OpusClip (April 2026, 2.88M clips).** 30-60 s is the most common bucket (40.1%), but this
  measures what users *made*, not how videos performed. [Data, not performance]
  https://www.bytecap.io/research/best-youtube-shorts-length
- **Adobe Express survey of 507 creators.** Creators favour under 30 s for views, shares and
  saves; this is opinion, not measurement. [Survey]
  https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/youtube-shorts-length-study
- **vidIQ (as summarized):** Shorts of 13 s or 60 s "tend to perform best". [Unverified]
  https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/
- **Retention context.** The average Short is now watched for about 16 s (Metricool, above).
  A 55 s explainer therefore needs exceptional retention to reach a high average percentage
  viewed. **Inference:** shorter versions of the same idea deserve a test.
- **Channel note.** `config/platforms.yaml` no longer targets TikTok. The "over 60 seconds while
  TikTok is a target" rule in `CLAUDE.md` therefore no longer forces length, and lengths under
  60 s can be tested.

### 1.5 Loops and rewatches

- Since 2025-03-31 every replay counts as a public view. [Official, via news]
  https://tech.yahoo.com/general/articles/youtube-changing-view-means-youtube-162942286.html
- Monetization and YPP use **engaged views**, not raw replays.
  https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted
- **No controlled test shows that seamless loops raise distribution.** Guides call loops "not a
  guaranteed algorithm hack". [Practitioner]
  https://faceless.so/blog/seamless-video-loop-guide-for-tiktok-reels
- **The mechanism is plausible.** A loop raises average percentage viewed, which YouTube names as
  a signal. Treat it as a test, not a rule.
- Loop design advice: the ending flows into the first line; no closing pause, musical cutoff or
  sign-off. [Practitioner] https://faceless.so/blog/seamless-video-loop-guide-for-tiktok-reels

## 2. Titles, descriptions and hashtags on Shorts

### 2.1 #Shorts and hashtags

- **#Shorts is not required.** Shorts are classified by aspect ratio and length.
  - Only **17% of trending Shorts titles** contain #Shorts (TunePocket, 10K+ trending Shorts).
    [Data] https://www.tunepocket.com/youtube-shorts-video-titles/
  - Including it costs nothing. [Practitioner]
    https://postlinkapp.com/blog/youtube-shorts-hashtags
- **Metricool 2026: Shorts with hashtags got about 10% fewer views but about 9% more
  engagement.** This is correlational. Metricool: hashtags help categorize but "won't make a
  video go viral". [Data] https://metricool.com/fr/hashtags-youtube/
- **YouTube hashtag rules (third-party descriptions of the policy).**
  - The first 3 hashtags show above the title on the watch page.
  - More than 60 hashtags makes YouTube ignore all of them. One site says 15; the figures
    conflict.

  [Practitioner citing policy] https://insights.vaizle.com/best-hashtags-for-youtube-shorts/ ;
  https://www.collabpals.com/tools/youtube-hashtag-generator
- **Practitioner consensus: 3-5 relevant tags,** a secondary signal at most.
  https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-shorts-hashtags-virality ;
  https://virlo.ai/blog/how-many-hashtags-should-i-use-on-youtube-shorts
- **Tags vs hashtags.** About 60.5% of trending Shorts use hashtags and about 24.8% use tags.
  [Data] https://www.tunepocket.com/should-i-still-use-tags-and-hashtags-on-youtube/
- **Broad vs niche.** The most common hashtags on trending Shorts are generic (#shorts, #fyp,
  #viral, #usa), but the sample was only 48 Shorts. No study compares broad and niche tags on
  YouTube. [Weak data]
  https://www.socialcrawl.dev/data/best-hashtags-for-youtube-shorts
  - Instagram itself says targeted tags work better than generic ones like #reels or #explore
    (see section 6).

### 2.2 Titles

- **Visible length.** The Shorts feed overlay shows roughly the first ~40 characters (third-party
  estimates of 40-50). [Practitioner] https://howmanywords.app/blog/youtube-title-character-limit
- **Trending Shorts titles are short:** after removing hashtags, mostly 4-6 words and 20-40
  characters. [Data] https://www.tunepocket.com/youtube-shorts-video-titles/
- **AIR Media-Tech 2026 title study (11 niches, correlational).** [Data]
  - 30-50 characters performs best in most niches.
  - Titles over 90 characters underperform.
  - Numbers help mainly in tech, business, fitness and food.

  https://air.io/en/audience-growth/how-to-write-a-youtube-title-that-gets-clicked-research-across-11-niches
- **TubeBuddy (second-hand):** 5-7 word titles outperform longer ones. [Unverified]
  https://www.tunepocket.com/youtube-shorts-video-titles/
- **Emojis: mixed evidence.**
  - vidIQ (128.5M videos uploaded September 2025): Shorts with an emoji in the title averaged
    +49% views, but the median gain was only +13%.
    https://vidiq.com/blog/post/emojis-in-youtube-titles/
  - OverseerOS matched each emoji title to one from the same channel, format and week; the
    effect disappeared. https://www.overseeros.com/blog/emojis-in-youtube-titles
  - Conclusion: optional, low priority.
- **No native A/B testing for Shorts titles.** YouTube's "A/B test titles & thumbnails" excludes
  Shorts. [Official] https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400?hl=en
- **Search.**
  - Search drives about 9% of all views (Metricool, above).
  - Guides advise the keyword in the first ~40 characters, spoken in the voiceover, and in the
    first description line. [Practitioner]
    https://www.clipspeed.ai/blog/youtube-shorts-seo-optimization-guide.html
  - The claim that "researched keywords get 40-60% more search impressions" is unsourced.
    [Unverified]
- **Misleading metadata is enforced.** The fake-trailer channels were terminated for "spam and
  misleading metadata". [Official, via news]
  https://www.netinfluencer.com/youtube-terminates-ai-generated-fake-movie-trailer-channels/

### 2.3 Descriptions

- The description is not shown in the Shorts feed. It matters for search, for context, and as
  the place for sources and disclosure. No study measures its effect on Shorts reach.
  [Practitioner] https://www.clipspeed.ai/blog/youtube-shorts-seo-optimization-guide.html

## 4. Hooks that raise first-seconds retention

**Evidence quality is low.** No public, peer-reviewed or platform-published dataset gives

What is supported:

1. **The first second decides the swipe.** Galloway: keep VVSA above 60%; 70-90% does best.
   [Data] https://videogen.io/blog/decoding-the-youtube-shorts-algorithm-a-deep-dive-into-3-3-billion-views
2. **Open on three channels at once.** Viral faceless Shorts open with a visual, an on-screen

   appeared in "virtually all" of them. [Vendor data]

3. **Kinds of hook** (Bytecap, synthesized from the literature):
   - result first;
   - precise question;
   - problem and solution;
   - story teaser;
   - surprising claim;
   - visual reveal.

   Bytecap's advice: "test categories against chose-to-view and early retention rather than
   declaring one universal winner". A surprising claim the clip cannot support backfires.
   https://www.bytecap.io/research/best-hooks-for-youtube-shorts

## 5. Risks and how to stay safe while posting several a day

### 5.1 Policies that apply to a faceless AI explainer channel

- **Inauthentic content (YPP, monetization).**
  - Official examples:
    - channels uploading many narrated stories with only superficial differences;
    - slideshow channels with identical narration;
    - AI content made with "generic or unoriginal templates" that "gives the impression of
      mass production".
  - "Allows creators to use AI tools … to enhance their storytelling".

  [Official, via news] https://ppc.land/youtube-clarifies-inauthentic-content-policy-changes/ ;
  https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/
- **The July 2026 clarification adds three categories:**
  - generic or repetitive content;
  - "unsatisfying or off-putting" content;
  - **AI personas presented as experts on sensitive topics, with AI financial "podcast hosts"
    named as an example.**

  Narrating researched content with an AI voice is distinguished from inventing a fake expert.
  It is a monetization rule, not a removal rule.
  https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/07/13/youtube-inauthentic-content-monetization-policy-update/ ;
  https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-youtube-ai-video-monetization-rules/
- **Review is channel-wide.** If enough of a channel is inauthentic, the whole channel can lose
  monetization; "faceless" alone is not the trigger. [Practitioner, consistent]
  https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage ;
  https://kompozy.io/guides/faceless-ai-video-channels-after-platform-crackdowns
- **Templated faceless creators are under pressure in 2026.**
  - The Next Web reports human faceless creators caught in the AI-slop purge.
  - A claim that 16 channels were terminated in January 2026 is **unverified**.

  https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage ;
  https://www.bottlerocketcontent.com/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-2026/
- **Originality and reach (October 2026).** Reach falls for re-uploads. Using only our own
  generated visuals avoids this; using third-party stock or gameplay footage would not.
  https://ppc.land/re-uploaded-shorts-lose-reach-as-youtube-favours-original-clips/

### 5.2 AI disclosure

- **Disclosure is required for realistic altered or synthetic content.** It is not required for
  clearly unrealistic or animated content, animation, effects, or using AI for scripts.
  [Official] https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content ;
  https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/youtube-ai-disclosure-in-2026-label-it-yourself-or-youtube-will
- **Since May 2026:**
  - the label is an on-screen overlay on Shorts for photorealistic or meaningfully AI content;
  - YouTube auto-labels undisclosed photorealistic AI;
  - **a label by itself does not change recommendation or monetization.**

  https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/improving-ai-labels-viewers-creators/ ;
  https://the-decoder.com/youtube-will-try-to-automatically-flag-ai-videos-starting-this-month/
- **What this means for our clay/3D style.** It is probably "clearly unrealistic", so disclosure
  is not strictly required. The repo already sets `contains_synthetic_media: true`, as
  `CLAUDE.md` requires. That carries no reach penalty, according to YouTube.
- **Unknown viewer-perception risk.** The March 2026 "Did this feel like AI slop?" survey
  suggests YouTube collects slop perception, and how it is used is undisclosed. Garbled text,
  morphing and uncanny artefacts likely hurt satisfaction. **Unverified mechanism.**
  https://pcworld.com/article/3096317/youtube-wants-you-to-help-spot-ai-slop-videos.html

### 5.3 Other risks

- **Music claims on Shorts over 60 s.** One claim blocks the video worldwide. The Shorts audio
  library places invisible claims, so use claim-free audio for Shorts over 60 s.
  https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en ;
  https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13053317 ;
  https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-shorts-copyright-guide
  - The repo's assembler synthesizes its own music bed. Keep it that way.
- **Misleading metadata or titles.** This is what terminated the fake-trailer channels. Titles
  must match what the video delivers.
  https://www.netinfluencer.com/youtube-terminates-ai-generated-fake-movie-trailer-channels/
- **YPP thresholds after 2027-02-01.** Entry needs 1,000 subscribers plus **20M** qualified
  Shorts views in 90 days, up from 10M today. Staying in the Shorts revenue pool needs 10M per
  rolling 90 days. Existing YPP members keep their status.
  https://ppc.land/youtube-doubles-monetization-thresholds-and-adds-a-rolling-shorts-test/
  - **Implication:** under the reach-first strategy, the cheaper bar is qualifying before
    February 2027. That is unlikely for a brand-new channel, so plan around the 20M bar.

### 5.4 Staying safe at 2-3 videos a day (synthesis of the above)

1. **Never ship two consecutive videos with the same structure and template.**
   - Rotate format (question explainer, what-if, money-math, micro-story, myth vs fact).
   - Rotate opening style, visual style and narrator.
   - Vary script length.
2. **Every video carries original substance:** a sourced fact set and a specific angle; not
   "the first search result read aloud".
   https://www.panstag.com/2026/08/is-youtube-demonetizing-faceless-videos.html
3. **No narrator persona with implied credentials,** especially in finance, credit, insurance
   and legal topics. Never "As a financial advisor…", never a named AI "money coach" character.
4. **Avoid shock or fear bait.** "Off-putting" content is non-monetizable.
5. **Use only our own generated visuals and synthesized audio.** No stock, gameplay or
   third-party clips.
6. **A human-quality check on artefacts** before posting: text, hands, morphing.
7. **Titles honest to the content.** Sources in the description.

## 8. Concrete recommendations for this channel

Each item is phrased as a test with a metric. The baseline is our own Shorts, not industry
numbers.

**Metrics:**

- YouTube Studio → Content → Shorts:
  - **Viewed vs swiped away (VVSA)**;
  - **average percentage viewed (APV)**;
  - **engaged views at 48 h and at 7 days**.
- Instagram: **sends per reach** and average watch time.
- Facebook: 3-second views and shares.

Change behaviour in `config/` first, as `CLAUDE.md` asks.

### 8.1 Target lengths to test (YouTube; reuse the same cut on Instagram and Facebook)

TikTok is not a target, so the 60-second floor no longer applies. Using the measured 2.9 words
a second (lessons.md), run three length arms, rotated so each niche gets each arm:

| Arm | Length | Script |
|---|---|---|
| **S** | **22-30 s** | 65-85 words, 3 beats: hook → one surprising fact or number → twist or loop line |
| **M** | **35-45 s** | 100-130 words, 4-5 beats |
| **L** | **50-58 s** | 145-165 words; the current default, kept as control |

- Hypothesis: S and M raise APV and VVSA with no loss of engaged views. It follows from EasyViral
  and Metricool's ~16 s average watch, and from Galloway's 50-60 s finding (2023).
- Decide after at least 6 videos per arm at the 7-day mark.
- **Only exceed 60 s** for story formats whose retention curve stays flat, and only with
  synthesized or claim-free audio.
- Instagram: 45-60 s is the best-measured band.
- Facebook: 60-90 s is fine. A Facebook-only longer cut is optional and low priority.

### 8.2 Hook rules and templates

Keep the existing rules: a spoken stake in the first five words, `on_screen_hook` for 2.6 s,
and a cut every 3.5 s or faster. Add:

1. **Frame 0 carries motion, text and voice together.** No leading silence or fade-in.
   (EasyViral)
2. **Rotate hook types and log the type in `script.json`,** so `pipeline insights` can compare
   VVSA by type:

| Hook type | Template | Example (needs a cited source) |
|---|---|---|
| Odd-object question | "Why is [everyday thing] [weird trait]?" | "Why do credit cards have 16 digits?" |
| What-if | "What happens if you [specific action]?" | "What happens if you only pay the minimum?" |
| Result-first number | "[Small amount] → [big result]. Here's the math." | "$200 a month became $500,000. Here's the math." |
| Person + paradox | "[Person], worth [number], [unexpected fact]." | "This man got rich from a job, not a company." |
| Myth flip | "[Common belief]? Not quite." | "Checking your credit hurts your score? Not quite." |
| Story teaser | "In [year], [person] [did one strange thing]." | "In 1938, a man sold the Eiffel Tower. Twice." (only if sourced) |
| Visual reveal | Silent striking clay shot plus a 3-word overlay | "Inside a $1 coin" |
| Brain fact | "Your brain [does X] when [Y]." | "Your brain treats a sale like a reward." (cite the study) |

3. **No fear or shock bait** in finance and health ("off-putting" risk), and no claim the video
   cannot back with a source.

### 8.3 Loop vs CTA ending (A/B)

- **Loop arm:** the last spoken line runs grammatically into the first line. No sign-off,
  `end_hold` about 0.3 s, music not cut.
- **CTA arm:** the current ending, "Follow for one money rule a day."
- Metrics: APV and engaged views per view.
- Evidence for loops is only plausible (APV is a named signal), so test it before adopting it.

### 8.4 Title templates (YouTube)

- At most 40 characters where possible, and keyword first.
- No running time, and no "#Shorts" in the title: only 17% of trending Shorts use it.
- At most one emoji, optional; the evidence is mixed.

Templates:

- `Why [Thing] Is [Weird Trait]`: "Why Airplane Windows Are Round"
- `What If You [Did X]?`: "What If You Never Paid Your Card?"
- `[Amount] a Day for [Years]`: "$5 a Day for 40 Years"
- `How [Product] Is Really Made`: "How Pencils Are Really Made"
- `The [Credit/Insurance] Rule Nobody Explains`: "The Deductible Rule Nobody Explains"
- `[Year]: The [Event] Nobody Remembers`: "1859: The Day the Sky Caught Fire"
- `Your Brain Does This When [Y]`
- `[Myth]? Not Quite.`

### 8.5 Description template (YouTube)

```
[One-sentence answer containing the main keyword, ≤100 chars]
[One extra fact or the number behind the claim]

Sources: [Source 1 short name] [URL] · [Source 2] [URL]
[Finance/insurance/credit/legal/health niches:] Educational explainer, not financial/legal/medical advice.
Visuals and narration made with AI tools.
#[niche-tag] #[topic-tag]
```

Instagram and Facebook captions:

- Keyword-rich first line of 150 characters or fewer, which is the existing `hook_line`.
- One value sentence.
- A soft share prompt aimed at sends ("Send this to someone who…"), since sends per reach is a

### 8.7 Formats to test

These fit the Higgsfield clay/3D keyframe pipeline. Rotate them so no two consecutive uploads
share a format.

1. **Odd-object explainer** ("Why is X like this?"). Clay object close-ups and a cutaway reveal;
   20-35 s. This is the closest proven format (Zack D. Films, Jared Owen). Niches: how things
   are made, money objects (cards, cheques, coins), insurance and credit mechanics.
2. **What-if scenario**, in space, science or finance: "What if you invested $1,000 at 18?"
   30-45 s, with a twist ending.
3. **Money math in one image.** One number and one visual metaphor (clay coins stacking, a jar
   filling); 20-30 s; result-first hook.
4. **History micro-story.** One person, one moment, a twist; 35-55 s. Keep it unrealistic and
   claymation to avoid photoreal "AI POV" misinformation optics, and cite sources.
5. **Myth vs fact.** Two beats (credit, insurance, psychology); 20-30 s.
6. **"Your brain does X."** A named study, one experiment recreated in clay; 30-40 s.
7. **Optional: top-3 countdown,** only 3 items and a payoff at number 1; 35-45 s.

Avoid:

- Reddit or gameplay split-screen, which uses others' footage.
- Photoreal AI POV, which needs a prominent label and carries misinformation optics.
- Any recurring named AI "expert" host.
- Stock-footage slideshows.

### 8.8 Posting volume

- **2-3 Shorts a day, all distinct,** within the existing cap of 3 in `config/production.yaml`.
  - Metricool: monthly Shorts views keep rising with volume, though each Short peaks at 2-4 a
    week.
  - The 300-in-30-days anecdote shows volume without quality does not work.
- Spread uploads at least 4-6 hours apart. At most one video per niche per day.
- At least 3 different formats and 2 narrators a week.
- **Evaluate at 48 h and again at 7 days.** Shorts often gain views late.
- **Kill rule:** if a format's VVSA stays below 60% across 5 videos, retire or rework it.
  - Double down where VVSA is above 70% and APV is above our median.
- Instagram and Facebook:
  - Post the same clean master, not a YouTube Studio download, which carries a watermark.
  - Each post needs a native caption.
  - Buffer's data supports daily Instagram posting with diminishing returns.
  - Use Trial Reels for hook tests once the account qualifies.

### 8.9 Safety checklist per video

- [ ] Original generated visuals only; no third-party clips or stock.
- [ ] Every number sourced in `sources`; the title matches the payoff.
- [ ] No narrator persona or credentials; the finance, insurance, legal or health disclaimer is
  present.
- [ ] No shock or fear bait; no garbled on-image text, morphing or uncanny hands.
- [ ] Synthesized or claim-free audio, which is mandatory above 60 s.
- [ ] `contains_synthetic_media: true` (no reach penalty per YouTube).
- [ ] This video's format, hook type and narrator differ from the previous upload's.
